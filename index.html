01 / 06BLUE ASH · 39.23°N

The corridor starts farther south than people think

Blue Ash ANGS, ~15 mi from downtown Cincinnati, has hosted the 123rd Air Control Squadron since 1953 — a unit of Springfield's 178th Wing. Command-and-control, not intelligence, but it stretches the corridor's military footprint to the Cincinnati metro.

[D] nationalguard.mil · ang.af.mil · ~300 Airmen home from deployment Dec 2025
02 / 06DOWNTOWN DAYTON · INNOVATION DISTRICT

The network layer lives downtown

Wright Brothers Institute runs AFRL's Small Business Hub at 444 E. Second St — the clearance-agnostic front door for small firms. AFCEA Dayton-Wright convenes the C4ISR crowd; UDRI holds an $850M AF rapid problem-solving contract (Mar 2026).

[D] wrightbrothersinstitute.org · daytonwrightafcea · Dayton Daily News
03 / 06WRIGHT-PATTERSON AFB · AREA A / B

The anchor: 38,000 people, $16B a year

Ohio's largest single-site workforce. NASIC (~4,100 analysts, >$507M budget) grades foreign air, space, missile and cyber threats; AFRL runs a ~$7B S&T portfolio; AFLCMC manages acquisition programs; the 655th ISR Wing (Reserve) drills on the same installation — its 14th IS does acquisition intelligence, its 64th IS supports NASIC directly.

[D] nasic.af.mil · 445aw.afrc.af.mil · DDC/JobsOhio study
04 / 06BEAVERCREEK / FAIRBORN · THE RING

Twenty firms within two road-miles of the fence

The contractor ring — Riverside Research, Apogee, KBR Wyle, SPA (the four $866M NASIC A&AS awardees, May 2026), Booz Allen's digital hub, Altamira, Leonardo DRS, SNC, and the small-firm layer. Positioned here for one reason: the customer is next door.

[D] DoD award announcements · verified place lookups · firm sites
05 / 06SPRINGFIELD-BECKLEY ANGB · 39.84°N

The Guard bridge

The 178th Wing — 926 assigned — flies MQ-9 missions and fields the 178th ISR Group (124th, 125th, 126th, 127th Intelligence Squadrons), which directly supports NASIC. One wing, two component paths into the same intelligence center as the Reserve's 655th.

[D] ong.ohio.gov · 178wing.ang.af.mil · Dayton Daily News

SEC 06 · METHOD
How this was built

What this is

An open-source mapping of the Wright-Patterson AFB / Dayton defense-intelligence ecosystem — institutions, contractors, money flows, and the network layer — built with ICD-203-style evidence discipline. Compiled Jul 2026; data refreshed Oct 2026.

Evidence grading

[D] Documented in a primary source or credible press account, cited in each card.
[I] Analytic inference — a reasoned conclusion, not a direct citation.
[V] Widely reported but not independently confirmed — sits in the Watchlist until checked.

Zero-fabrication rule

No organization, contract, or program on this map is invented. Where a detail is properly non-public (e.g. classified NASIC mission specifics), the card says so rather than speculating.

Scope and OPSEC

Publicly released information only — official fact sheets, press releases, contract announcements, and organization websites. Deliberately excludes personnel names, building-level operational detail, unit manning beyond published figures, and mission specifics past the public structure. Aggregation of open sources can itself create sensitivity; the exclusions are a choice, not an oversight.

Components

Guard (ANG) and Reserve (AFRC) render as dashed pillars and rings — part-time components, distinct from full-time institutions, with different chains of command and accession paths.

Known gaps

  • Classified contract actions are invisible to every public source used here.
  • DoD award data lags ~90 days on USASpending.
  • Four national primes' Dayton footprints remain [V] pending a USASpending pull.
  • OTA consortium fees and current rosters are not published centrally.
  • Interstates are illustrative routings drawn through verified city coordinates, not traced highway geometry. Brass arcs are relationship links, not roads.

Geometry

Site coordinates verified by live place lookup, Jul 2026; reference-city coordinates (city halls / municipal centers, 18 cities) verified Oct 2026. The 3D terrain is stylized; relative site positions are true (equirectangular, longitude scaled by cos latitude). WPAFB and the Beavercreek ring are genuinely ~2 road-miles apart — the map does not spread them for looks.

Data and source

Everything on this page is rendered from plain JSON files, published beside it under CC BY 4.0: sites · network · contracts · watchlist · geography · money. Code is MIT-licensed on GitHub, with a validator that checks every reference before anything is published.
